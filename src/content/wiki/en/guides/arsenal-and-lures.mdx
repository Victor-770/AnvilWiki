## What is in the How to Fish gun cabinet?

Six tools of indoor-outdoor marine conflict. Note what's **missing** from this table: a reason to buy them before your lures and grill. Guns convert catches into chips; they do not create catches.

| Weapon | Cost | Role | Field note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter Pistol | Free | Emergency sidearm | Yours at the Island 1 dock. Loud, weak, loyal. |
| Shotgun | $180 | Swarm answer | Wide cone, short temper. The Island 2 purchase. |
| SMG | $340 | Sustained fire | Leech drones and flying-fish schools. Rewards trigger discipline. |
| Assault Rifle | $720 | Precision | Mid-air calls at range and Island 3+ duels. |
| Dynamite ×3 | $150 | Area denial | Fishing, if you hate fishing. Never stacks with trick shots. |
| Harpoon Gun | $950 | Boss utility | Pins big fish for the reel. The Reef Tyrant hates it here. |

<Callout type="tip" title="Trick shots">
Trick shots pay **5×** on airborne catches with any gun except dynamite. The 360 adds nothing to the payout and everything to the highlight reel. Do it anyway.
</Callout>

## What does every lure do?

Lures are the real economy of How to Fish. Each one unlocks a family of species — which means each one is an income unlock before it is a fishing preference. The per-species pairings are in the [fishipedia](/fish/fishipedia-species-database/).

| Lure | Cost | Effect | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Hook | Free | No modifiers | Sardine, herring, whatever is desperate |
| Bright Spinner | $45 | Bite rate up on schoolers | Mackerel, bluegill, first-day income |
| Worm Ball | $60 | Bounces with the current | Mudskippers and tide-pool tricksters |
| Meat Chunk | $80 | Predator aggro | Piranha, catfish, post-swarm windows |
| Feather Rig | $120 | Spooks schools upward | Flying fish — the trick-shot economy |
| Steel Leader | $140 | Stops line cuts | Sharks, turtles, anything with teeth |
| Glow Lure | $160 | Lights up night feeders | Anglerfish, lantern squid, night cycles |
| Deep Troll Rig | $260 | Long-drop line | Swordfish, manta rays, the Abyss floor |
| Golden Hook | $600 | Drip-tier attraction | Drippy urchins and high-roller payouts |
| Mythic Lure | $2,000 | Summons endgame spawns | Comet tuna, leviathan fry, ghost marlin |

## What is the upgrade order that actually scales?

The shop sells everything from day one, which is exactly how it gets you. The community-tested order:

1. **Bright spinner ($45)** — cheapest bite-rate multiplier in the game.
2. **Second grill slot** — food security is DPS security. The hunger math is in the [cooking guide](/guides/cooking-and-recipes/).
3. **Shotgun ($180)** — the Swarm's leech waves are unmanageable without it.
4. **Feather rig ($120)** — unlocks flying fish, where trick-shot money lives.
5. **SMG ($340)** — sustained fire for drones; the shotgun's reload gets someone killed.
6. **Steel leader ($140)** — before Island 4, unless you enjoy re-tying lines mid-shark.
7. **Assault rifle ($720) + deep troll rig ($260)** — the Island 3/4 dueling combo.
8. **Golden hook ($600)** — the drippy urchin trade funds everything after this.
9. **Harpoon gun ($950)** — boss utility; one charge per voyage, so make it count.
10. **Boat engine** — a luxury until the map gets long, which is exactly Island 4.
11. **Mythic lure ($2,000)** — the endgame key. Everything else is cosmetics.

<Callout type="warn" title="Difficulty deltas">
On **Hard mode**, swarm pressure arrives a day early — move the shotgun to slot two and treat the SMG as mandatory before Island 3. On **Easy**, you can skip the SMG entirely and ride shotgun momentum to the Golden Isle.
</Callout>

## Lines, traps and the boring drawer

The shop's third shelf sells line upgrades, traps and bait bulk packs. Most of it is skippable — except three items the community treats as mandatory:

- **Insulated line** (Island 2) — electric eels otherwise cost more in reel durability than they pay.
- **Tide pool trap** (Island 1) — crabs sell steadily while you do literally anything else.
- **Reinforced trap** (Island 5) — the Abyssal King Crab needs two crew and this trap, or it needs a new boat.

Everything else in the drawer is bait bulk, which is fine value and zero fun to manage. Buy the 20-pack, forget about it, and spend your attention on the fish that actually fight back. When those fights are bosses rather than fish, the [boss guides](/bosses/) cover which gun each one punishes you for skipping.
