## How does the chip conversion work?

Dollars buy gear; chips buy only casino things. That's the whole trap, printed right on the exchange window in small text: **chips never convert back to dollars**. Every chip you spin is money that will never buy a grill slot again. Convert with a budget already decided — never "see how it goes".

<Callout type="warn" title="Island 5 inverts the rule">
On **Island 5** the boardwalk shops flip it: certain endgame purchases only take chips. From that island onward the casino is infrastructure, not entertainment — budget accordingly.
</Callout>

## What are the slot cabinet odds?

Three reels, seven symbols, one jackpot animation designed to be screenshotted. The community has spun enough sessions to reverse-engineer rough odds:

| Result | Rough odds | Payout | House edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Three fish (small win) | ~1 in 8 | 1.5× spin | always negative |
| Three urchins (solid win) | ~1 in 25 | 4× spin | always negative |
| Three sevens? No — three boots | ~1 in 60 | 10× spin | always negative |
| Golden sardine jackpot | ~1 in 400 | 777× spin + the fish itself | almost fair, somehow |

Every line is a long-term loss except the jackpot, which the community swears is "almost fair" — which is exactly what a house would want people to say. Set a chip budget, enjoy the lights, and treat the **Jackpot!** achievement (tracked with the rest of them in the [achievement hunt](/guides/achievement-hunt/)) as a twenty-minute tax you pay exactly once.

## Why are charms the one slot machine that respects you?

Charms are casino-bought passives that stack **+10% payout each**, and they persist for the whole session. This is the only casino interaction with a guaranteed return:

- **One charm is an investment.** It pays itself back over a normal island's worth of sales.
- **Five charms is a Hard-mode requirement.** On the +40% payout curve, a full charm stack compounds into real money.
- **Charms apply after grilled bonuses** — stack multiplicatively, not additively.

<Callout type="tip" title="High Roller timing">
The **High Roller** achievement wants all charms at once. Buy them on Hard mode, where the fatter payouts make the stack cheapest to assemble.
</Callout>

## How does the high-roller table work?

The back-room buyer pays a **drip premium** on rare species — urchin tier and up, boss trophies included. This is the second always-correct interaction:

1. Catch the rare fish like normal (golden hook, mythic lure, boss fights — the [island walkthrough](/guides/island-walkthrough/) maps which islands hold them).
2. **Do not sell at the dock scale.** Walk it to the back room.
3. Collect roughly 30–60% more than dock price, scaling with rarity.

A Pufferfish Prime trophy at the high-roller table is the single largest legal income event in the game. The **Drip Premium** achievement unlocks the first time you do this, and it should be the first casino achievement you chase — no variance, no odds, just a better scale.

## What is the third honest interaction?

Betting on **dockside fish fights** — two locals, one pool, one winner. The odds board is honest and the favorite usually wins; it's the closest thing the casino has to a savings account. The returns are small, the sessions are short, and the achievement for betting on the underdog and winning (**Long Shot**) is the only reason to ever deviate.

## What are the house rules for crews?

Four rules that keep a lobby solvent through the whole run:

1. **The Captain holds the purse.** Crew spending is a team decision, especially after the third "one more spin" — the [crew roles guide](/guides/co-op-crew-roles/) puts the purse firmly in the Captain's hands.
2. **Charm purchases beat spin purchases** every single time. There is no counterexample.
3. **Sell rare at the back room, common at the dock.** The drip premium only applies to rarity tiers.
4. **Set the chip budget before entering.** The cabinet is not an investment strategy. Probably.

That's the economy. The casino is a tool with three useful teeth and a beautifully lit money furnace in the middle — run the teeth, admire the furnace, keep the receipts.
