## What keeps a crew alive in How to Fish?

Your hunger bar drains constantly and drains **faster while sprinting, reeling and fighting**. Every fish you catch is potential food, but raw fish restore very little — the grill is where the value is. The rhythm that works: fish in bursts of 3–4 casts, then hand the haul to whoever owns the stove. A crew that grills in batches never thinks about hunger again; a crew that grills one fish at a time is always one wave away from starving mid-fight.

<Callout type="tip" title="Grill fatty fish">
Grill **fatty fish** (mackerel and up) and keep small sardines as emergency snacks. High-value fish are worth more sold than eaten — with two exceptions when a boss fight is imminent. The full ingredient table lives in the [cooking & recipes guide](/guides/cooking-and-recipes/).
</Callout>

## How does the grill rotation work?

Four habits cover it. Get them right and hunger stops being a mechanic you think about:

1. **One person owns the stove.** Usually the Chef, but on a duo run just pick whoever reels worse. The four [co-op crew roles](/guides/co-op-crew-roles/) exist exactly for this split.
2. **Grill in batches of four.** The stove fits four fillets; starting a batch for one fish wastes the cycle.
3. **Flip at the sizzle change.** Burnt fish restore half the hunger. There is no achievement for charcoal.
4. **Seagulls target active grills.** If a raid alarm sounds mid-batch, someone covers the stove or the batch becomes bird food.

## When do fish bite — day, night and storms?

Fish bite on loose cycles that change per island. Days favor surface schoolers like sardines and mackerel; nights wake up the glow-feeders (anglerfish, and on later islands, the fish you actually came for). The mistake almost every new crew makes is treating night as downtime — it's the **highest-value fishing window** if your lure box has a glow rig in it.

- **Dawn/dusk** — best raw catch rate for income fish. Sell first, upgrade second.
- **Night** — rare species window. Bring glow lures and a flashlight for the deck.
- **Storms** — bites drop to near zero. Use storms to reorganize the tackle box, cook, and plan routes.

The full species-by-timeslot pairing is in the [fishipedia](/fish/fishipedia-species-database/).

## Which mistakes end runs?

Three, and they are all shopping mistakes rather than fishing mistakes:

1. **Buying weapons before a grill upgrade.** The second grill slot pays for itself within a day of game time; the second gun does not. The [arsenal & lures guide](/guides/arsenal-and-lures/) has the full upgrade order.
2. **Parking the boat on the school.** Propeller noise scatters fish — idle in neutral about two boat-lengths off the spot and cast in.
3. **Fighting hunger with dynamite.** Blowing up a school does kill fish, and about a third of them are even recoverable. It is never the right trade.

<Callout type="warn" title="The blackout drift">
If the hunger bar fully empties at sea, you don't die — you **black out and drift**, and the drift has a bad habit of ending inside Island 3 territory. Always keep one cooked stack in the pantry as a parachute.
</Callout>

## What is a good first-day script?

Catch, cook, sell, upgrade — in that order, one purchase at a time:

1. Cast the basic rod at the nearest school and catch until the grill is full.
2. Cook everything, eat to full, sell the surplus at the dock.
3. Buy the **bright spinner** lure before any weapon. It doubles your bite rate on mackerel.
4. Upgrade the grill slot with the second payout.
5. Only then look at the shotgun — you'll want it for the Swarm.

That's the whole economy of the early game: catch → cook → sell → one upgrade at a time. Everything else in this guide library is just this loop with bigger numbers — the [island walkthrough](/guides/island-walkthrough/) is where the numbers start climbing.
