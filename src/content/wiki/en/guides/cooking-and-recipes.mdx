## How does the hunger loop work?

Your hunger bar drains constantly and drains **faster while sprinting, reeling and fighting**. Raw fish restore very little — the grill is where the value is. The rhythm that works: fish in bursts of 3–4 casts, then hand the haul to whoever owns the stove. A crew that grills in batches never thinks about hunger again; a crew that grills one fish at a time is always one wave away from starving mid-fight.

On **Easy** the drain is gentle enough that solo grazing works. On **Hard** the drain runs roughly a third faster and sprinting genuinely costs — the herring rations stack stops being optional the moment you leave Island 2.

## What is the full recipe book?

Eight recipes, unlocked by catching their ingredients — the game never explains this, which is why half the community thinks the grill only makes "grilled fish". It doesn't.

| Recipe | Ingredients | Effect | Field note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grilled Sardine | 2× sardine | Small hunger refill | The bread and butter. Batch four at a time. |
| Crispy Mackerel | 2× mackerel | Medium refill + stamina tick | First recipe that justifies the second grill slot. |
| Herring Rations | 4× herring | Four-stack, slow eat | Boss-fight picnic basket. Cook before you sail. |
| Eel Skewer | 1× electric eel | Hunger drains 20% slower, 10 min | Handle with the insulated glove or the Chef learns lightning. |
| Piranha Ceviche | 3× piranha | Big stamina spike, tiny HP nibble | The nibble is flavor. Allegedly. |
| Shark Kebab | 1× reef shark steak | Full bar + brief "fearless" | Leeches ignore you for ten seconds. Use them well. |
| Urchin Sushi | 1× drippy urchin | Medium refill | Feeds less than it earns. A flex, not a meal plan. |
| Golden Tartare | 1× golden sardine | 24h hunger lock | Also unlocks the "Waste of Money" achievement. Worth it. |

Where the ingredients live and which lure gets the bite: the [fishipedia](/fish/fishipedia-species-database/). What the buffed payouts are worth at the counter: [Casino Economics](/guides/casino-economics/).

## What is the grill rotation?

Four rules, and the first one is a staffing decision:

1. **One person owns the stove.** Usually the Chef, but on a duo run just pick whoever reels worse — the [crew roles guide](/guides/co-op-crew-roles/) explains why the Chef outranks the Gunner.
2. **Grill in batches of four.** The stove fits four fillets; starting a batch for one fish wastes the cycle.
3. **Flip at the sizzle change.** Burnt fish restore half the hunger. There is no achievement for charcoal.
4. **Cook for the next island, not this one.** Rations stack, hunger doesn't wait for the loading screen.

<Callout type="tip" title="Sell vs. eat">
Grill **fatty fish** (mackerel and up) for real meals and keep small sardines as emergency snacks. High-value fish are worth more sold than eaten — the recipe book's rare entries are the only exceptions.
</Callout>

## How do you win the seagull wars?

Seagulls target **active grills** — the fuller the batch, the harder they commit. A raid plays out in three beats: the cry (about two seconds of warning), the dive, and the heist. The counters, in order of effectiveness:

- **Slam the pot lid on.** The lid blocks a dive entirely; it costs one flip's timing.
- **One warning shot** — the shotgun scatters the flock for the rest of the day cycle. Costs shells; buys peace.
- **The scarecrow decoy** — a dock purchase nobody recommends until their third stolen batch.

Since patch 1.0.4, raid timing pauses while players are in menus — the birds got polite, not harmless (patch history is in the [patch notes roundup](/guides/patch-notes/)). If you hear the cry mid-boss, let the batch burn: losing four fillets loses less than dropping Pufferfish Prime's line.

## What is pantry doctrine?

Three habits that decide more voyages than any gun purchase:

1. **One cooked stack, always.** The hunger blackout doesn't kill you — it drifts the boat, and the drift has a bad habit of ending inside Island 3 territory.
2. **Rations before bosses.** Four herring rations feed a crew through any fight in the game.
3. **Cook the cheap stuff, sell the expensive stuff.** Urchin sushi is a flex; grilled sardines are a strategy.

That's the whole kitchen. It is the least glamorous system on the boat and the one that decides the most voyages — which is exactly why the Chef role outranks the Gunner in every serious lobby.
